You can't solve a meaning problem with just another achievement. I see this mistake all the time in people who have worked very hard to build good lives. Maybe that's you. You built the career. You raised the family. You hit many of the goals that you set for yourself. But then, at a certain point, the questions, well, they start to change. What comes next? What do I want to do now? And what do I want my life to be about right now? What do I want to be remembered for? What is all this actually for? Those are basically questions of meaning. The science tells us that meaning has three parts. Coherence, purpose, and significance. Coherence is understanding why your life is unfolding the way that it is. Purpose is knowing what your life is pointed toward, and significance is knowing why your life matters, and to whom. You can understand these ideas intellectually pretty quickly, but understanding them and actually building your life around them, well, those are two different things.
